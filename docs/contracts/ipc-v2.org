lives in the consumer repository, orris, and is vendored verbatim below rather
than restated, because a restatement is a second contract that drifts silently.

repository shipped only the v2 fixture bytes, so the producer had no copy of the
document whose frames it emits and no revision of the consumer was pinned in

fixtures; the fixtures half was already provable here and the paired-revision

*** What the drift control can and cannot see
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: what-the-drift-control-can-and-cannot-see
:END:
=vendored_ipc_v2_test.exs= fails when the vendored region is edited without
updating both pins, when the pins disagree, when the pinned fixture hash stops
matching the fixtures this repository ships, or when the fixture inventory named
here stops matching the directory.

It CANNOT see a change made in orris. Nothing in this repository can: the gate
has no access to the other repository. A new orris revision is adopted by
re-vendoring the bytes and updating =source_revision=, =source_sha256= and the
pin in the test, which is one reviewed change rather than a silent divergence.
Because the fixtures and their =CONTRACT_HASH= are byte-identical by design, a
one-sided fixture edit already fails the other repository gate; this document
adds the same property to the contract text.

** Producer addenda
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: producer-addenda
:END:
These statements are about THIS producer and are deliberately outside the
vendored region, so that they cannot be mistaken for the consumer contract and
cannot perturb its digest.

- A duplicate and a conflict are two different reply shapes, and the vendored
  text does not distinguish them. Admission answering =duplicate= produces an
  OK reply, =ok: true= with =duplicate: true= beside the receipt view. Admission
  answering =conflict= produces a NOT-OK reply, =ok: false= with
  =error: "conflict"= and the identities the caller presented, carrying no
  receipt view. A client that reads "ambiguous or conflict becomes durable
  attention" must therefore handle two shapes, not one.
- =invalid_msg_id= and =invalid_pane_id= are now named by the vendored text, in
  "Identity grammars and their refusals". Until orris =e5da392e= they were not,
  and this addendum carried them; what it carries instead is the producer fact
  that text asks for. This daemon has ONE =pane_id= predicate,
  =AiPair.Delivery.ReceiptLog.valid_pane?/1=, and the echo consults that same
  function rather than a second regex of its own, so a pane id this daemon
  echoes is a pane id it can store. The divergence the vendored text describes
  ("a daemon whose echo predicate is wider than its storage predicate will echo
  a pane id it then refuses") existed here and was measured on three hyphenated
  classes before it was closed.
- =not_delivered= is never answered as a duplicate, because admission opens the
  next attempt instead. The producer applies no attempt ceiling of its own; the
  retry bound is the consumer obligation the vendored text describes.

** Implementation status
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: implementation-status
:END:
The consumer side of this contract is delivered. ~AiOrchestrator.Dispatch.LocalPane~
(~lib/ai_orchestrator/dispatch/local_pane.ex~) sends and reconciles with
~protocol_version~ 2 and fails closed unless the ~ping~ capability preflight lists
~delivery_reconcile~; ~AiOrchestrator.Dispatch.PaneClient~
(~lib/ai_orchestrator/dispatch/pane_client.ex~) issues ~reconcile~ with
~--protocol-version 2~, refuses a reply that lacks ~protocol_version~ 2
(~protocol_version_unsupported~) and refuses a reply whose ~msg_id~ or ~pane_id~ echo does
not match (~reply_identity_*~ / ~reply_pane_*~). The fixture bytes and
~CONTRACT_HASH~ under ~test/fixtures/contracts/ipc/v2/~ are pinned by
~test/contracts/ipc_v2_contract_hash_test.exs~ and exercised by
~test/dispatch/ipc_v2_send_reply_test.exs~. The daemon that produces these frames
is a separate project; whether a given daemon answers version 2 is discovered at
runtime through the capability preflight, never assumed.

Status: the fixture bytes in ~test/fixtures/contracts/ipc/v2/~ are the v2 reply
shapes this repository pins, with their ~CONTRACT_HASH~ computed under the v1 hash
rule (see ~ipc-v1.org~). The v1 set and its hash are untouched; v1 remains the wire
until a client asks for version 2.

** Framing and version
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: framing-and-version
:END:
- A request that carries ~"protocol_version": 2~ is a v2 request. Every v2 reply
  carries ~"protocol_version": 2~ and ~ok~, and every v2 reply to a message-bound
  command echoes both ~msg_id~ and ~pane_id~. A reply without the version, or a v1
  reply to a v2 request, is not a v2 answer and a client MUST refuse it.
- ~ping~ with ~protocol_version~ 2 answers ~capabilities~, a list of tokens.
  ~delivery_reconcile~ is the token that authorizes a client to send at all
  (the client fails closed without it: it cannot ask what became of a send).
- A v2 ~send~ MUST carry ~msg_id~ (the client's send id); the daemon hashes the
  text it is given itself, so a send carries no payload hash and ~missing_msg_id~
  is the refusal. ~reconcile~ carries the ~payload_hash~ (~sha256:~ + 64 lowercase
  hex over the exact bytes) and is refused with ~missing_payload_hash~ without it.
- ~reconcile~ carries ~msg_id~, ~payload_hash~, ~pane_id~; the answer is one of
  exactly five ~outcome~ values: ~delivered~, ~queued~, ~absent~, ~ambiguous~,
  ~conflict~. ~not_delivered~ is a stored status that answers ~absent~; ~pending~
  answers ~ambiguous~ after the daemon's bounded wait. ~absent~ and ~conflict~
  carry no receipt view; ~conflict~ echoes the identities the caller presented,
  never the stored ones.

** Identity grammars and their refusals
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: identity-grammars-and-their-refusals
:END:
- ~msg_id~ is ~snd_~ followed by exactly 64 lowercase hex characters (the
  SEND-ID-1 digest; the minting rule is the client's, the grammar is shared).
- ~pane_id~ is ~%~ followed by 1..128 characters from ~[a-zA-Z0-9_]~. This is the
  GOVERNING grammar: it is the one the daemon's receipt record is keyed by, so a
  pane id outside it cannot be stored and therefore cannot be delivered. A daemon
  whose echo predicate is wider than its storage predicate will echo a pane id it
  then refuses; the two predicates are one grammar and a daemon reconciles them on
  its own side.
- An identity that fails its grammar is refused ~invalid_msg_id~ or
  ~invalid_pane_id~ BEFORE admission. The daemon drops the echo for the identity
  it could not read -- there is nothing safe to echo -- so these replies carry at
  most the other identity, and a client MUST NOT require an echo before naming
  them. They are request errors, facts about the request the client built, and are
  distinct from ~missing_msg_id~ / ~missing_pane_id~, which say the field was
  absent rather than malformed.

** Reconcile replies
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: reconcile-replies
:END:
~reconcile.<outcome>.json~ for the five outcomes; ~delivered~, ~queued~ and
~ambiguous~ carry the receipt view (~status~, ~delivery_attempt~, identities).
~absent~ comes in two shapes: no record at all (~reconcile.absent.json~, echoed
identities only, which a client reads as attempt 0) and a stored absence
(~reconcile.absent.not_delivered.json~: ~status~ ~not_delivered~ with the
positive ~delivery_attempt~ it closed) -- the client's retry bound depends on
that distinction, so a stored receipt without a positive attempt is refused.
~conflict~ carries only the identities the caller presented.

** Placeholders
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: placeholders
:END:
~<msg_id>~, ~<pane_id>~, ~<payload_hash>~ and ~<version>~ are dynamic and are
substituted by the consumer tests; everything else is exact bytes.
